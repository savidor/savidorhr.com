Guided calling

No random calls, and no lead quietly written off

A pipeline is only worth what somebody rings. The CRM hands each caller one lead at a time, walks them through the conversation, and will not let the call end without a date on the next step.

  • The calls double as market research. Where people want to buy and at what budget, captured as you go, including the demand you currently cannot meet.

The routing engine

Different rules per site, without a developer

A property under a joint venture may need the Managing Director on every request. A workshop may need two vendor quotes over a quarter million naira. Both are rows in a table your IT manager edits.

  • First approver overrides. Replace the raiser's own supervisor for a specific site or category.
  • Ordered approval flow. Insert or remove a stage and every future request follows the new path.
  • Visibility rules. Control which departments can even see a site in the request form.
  • Price hiding. Decide whether staff price their own items or procurement sources them.

Guided calling

The queue decides who is called next, not the caller

A caller opens the queue and gets one lead. They cannot browse, cherry pick the easy names or skip the awkward ones without saying why. Skips are recorded.

  • Promises come first. A callback someone was promised outranks everything. Then today's due calls, then leads never rung at all, newest first, because speed to first contact is the single biggest lever on conversion.
  • Five attempts, at five different times. The ladder spreads attempts across mornings, afternoons and evenings over two weeks. Five attempts all made at ten in the morning are one attempt made five times, and they miss the same person five times.
  • It is their list, not the company's. Callers see leads they own, are assigned, or have been handed. A telemarketer never lands on a lead another consultant has in negotiation.

The script

Your best caller's conversation, available to everybody

The call script is stored as a branching graph, not printed on a laminated card. The caller sees the question to ask, taps what the person actually said, and the system decides what comes next.

  • No call ends in nothing. Every possible answer leads either to another question or to an outcome that sets a date. Only do not call and wrong number close a lead permanently.
  • Parked is not pending. Someone who wants a house you do not sell is not a callback in four months. They are parked against the day you have it, and counted in demand.
  • Edited without a developer. Questions, answers and outcomes are all configuration. When the pitch changes, an administrator changes it once and every caller has it on the next call.
  • The next step becomes real work. An inspection goes to the lead's owner, a callback to the person who promised it. It appears on their task list, not in a note nobody reads.

For the ED and MD

Money that is signed, kept apart from money that is hoped for

Ask most CRMs what the pipeline is worth and you get one number that quietly adds a signed contract to a salesperson's optimism. Leadership gets two numbers here, and they are never added together.

  • Committed is real. Traced through the signed sale to the payment schedule, so it carries actual instalment dates and what has already been paid against them.
  • Expected is labelled a forecast. Open deals weighted by the odds of their stage, on a screen that says in plain words that this is a projection.
  • Leadership is not asked to configure anything. Thresholds, rules and settings belong to your administrator. The executive view answers when money is coming and from whom.
  • Raise a concern against the numbers. Tag a figure or a group of leads with a question, and it stays open until everyone asked has actually answered, not just the first person to reply.

Automation

The work that happens when nobody is looking

All of it ships switched off. Nothing routes, nudges or reassigns until somebody writes the rule and turns it on, because a CRM that starts moving leads on day one is a CRM nobody trusts by day three.

Leads route themselves

Rules match on property, source and budget band, then hand the lead out by rotation, to whoever is carrying least, or to a named person.

Approvals that stall get chased

An approval sitting too long reminds the person holding it. At double the threshold it escalates a level, so nothing waits on one inbox indefinitely.

Rot is found before it matters

Leads going cold, deals not moving stage, tasks due today and tomorrow's inspections all surface on their own.

Nobody is nagged twice

A reminder fires once per thing per day no matter how often the checks run. The usual reason people switch automation off is that it shouted.

Paid social leads arrive instantly

Lead forms from your ad accounts come in by signed webhook, checked for authenticity, deduplicated, and the marketer is told at once. CSV import covers the same path when you would rather not connect the account.

Calls become follow ups

A call that ends with a next action writes the task itself, owned by the right person, with the date already on it.

The unglamorous parts

Why the data is still trustworthy in year three

One person, one record

Phone numbers are compared as full international numbers, so a Lagos number and a London number that happen to share ten digits stay two different people. The warning you get while typing uses the same rule that blocks the save.

History that does not move

A deal keeps the currency it was agreed in and the exchange rate as it stood that day. Last year's report reads the same today as it did when it was signed.

A bad rate is parked, not applied

Rates refresh daily, but a jump beyond a set threshold is held for a human to accept or reject. A feed having a bad day cannot silently reprice your pipeline.

"Why can I not see that lead?"

Your administrator picks any user and sees their role, every permission and what they can reach, without signing in as them or guessing.

Permission down to the person

Roles cover most people. For the rest, an individual can be granted or denied a single capability, or limited to certain tabs, and every change is logged with who made it.

Help where the question is

A guide per module written for the reader's role, a note on the fields people get wrong, and empty screens that explain how work arrives rather than saying there is nothing here.

Questions we get asked first

Why is there no price on this page?

Because a headcount of 60 and a headcount of 600 are different products, and a company switching on four modules is not buying what a group switching on twenty four is buying. Publishing one number would be wrong for almost everybody reading it. Tell us the two facts and you get a real figure, not a range.

Can we start small and add modules later?

Yes, and most companies do. Modules switch on without a migration because they already share one permission model and one employee record. You are quoted the difference, not a new licence.

How is a group with several arms priced?

Once, across all the arms in scope. You are not quoted separately per company, and staff who work across arms are counted once.

What does the setup fee actually cover?

Mapping your existing approval chains into the routing engine, importing your people and their reporting lines, and training the administrators. It is the work that decides whether the system matches how you already operate.

What happens to our data if we leave?

Full export on request, in a format you can read without us. The break glass console also snapshots any table before a destructive operation.

The opportunity

Software the incumbents will not sell to

The market

African firms of 50 to 1,000 staff are numerous, formalising quickly, and structurally underserved. Global ERP vendors price and scope for organisations with a dedicated IT function, which these companies do not have.

Why now

Lender and regulatory pressure is pushing mid market firms toward auditable record keeping, while cheap cloud hosting and near universal smartphone ownership have removed the last infrastructure barrier.

The moat

The routing engine. Competitors ship one fixed approval path. Every company onboarded here deepens the library of approval patterns the product ships with, so each deployment is faster than the last.

The model

High margin one time setup fees fund acquisition. Annual licences by headcount band compound. Premium modules expand revenue inside an existing account with no additional sales effort.

Use of funds

Multi tenancy and self service onboarding to remove the services bottleneck, a commercial team for the Lagos and Abuja mid market, and the security controls needed to clear procurement review at larger accounts.

What exists today

A complete, deployed system with audit logging, push notification, email delivery and a configurable routing engine already in production, rather than a prototype seeking validation.

Why it defends itself

Approval infrastructure is hard to rip out

Once a company's real approval chains, budgets and audit history live in one system, replacing it means re documenting how the business makes decisions. That is a far higher switching cost than a departmental tool.

  • It sits across departments. Finance, HR, sales and operations all depend on the same records, so no single department can remove it.
  • The audit trail accumulates. Its value grows with age, and it cannot be migrated to a competitor without losing the history.
  • Expansion is natural. Companies start with requisitions, then add HR, then revenue, without a new procurement cycle.

Questions we expect

Is this a product or a services business today?

Today revenue mixes a one time deployment engagement with an annual licence. The use of funds is explicitly aimed at shifting that mix toward licence revenue by building multi tenancy and self service onboarding, which is what removes the services bottleneck on growth.

What stops a global vendor moving down market?

Price and process. Serving a 200 person Nigerian group profitably requires a deployment model measured in weeks and priced in naira. That is a different business from an enterprise ERP sale, and incumbents have repeatedly chosen not to build it.

How defensible is a workflow product technically?

The code is not the moat. The accumulated library of real approval patterns is, alongside the switching cost of a live audit history that spans every department in a company.

What is the concentration risk?

Real and acknowledged. The product is proven inside one multi arm group, which is a strong reference but a single one. Broadening the customer base is the primary objective of this round.
